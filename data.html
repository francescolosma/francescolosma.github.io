Data

A growing collection of datasets I have come across in my research: where to find them, their main variables, and (a small list of) papers that use them. Suggestions are welcome at francesco.losma@economics.ox.ac.uk.

Public Datasets

National Longitudinal Survey of Youth 1979 (NLSY79)

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics · United States · 12,686 people born 1957–1964, interviewed every year from 1979 to 1994 and every two years since · Public microdata, geographic detail on application

Main variables: week-by-week employment histories, wages and earnings, education and training, family background, marriage, fertility and child care, health, assets and income, and participation in government programmes; cognitive test scores (ASVAB, from which the AFQT is derived) taken in 1980. A companion survey follows the respondents' children (NLSY79 Child and Young Adult).

Used in: Deming (2017), Guvenen, Kuruscu, Tanaka and Wiczer (2020), Lise and Postel-Vinay (2020)

Occupational Information Network (O*NET)

U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration · United States · About 900 occupations, updated quarterly · Public, free download

Main variables: for each occupation, ratings of the skills, abilities and knowledge it requires, its work activities, work context and work styles, the education, training and experience needed, task statements, and the tools and technology used. Often merged with worker surveys such as the NLSY79 to measure the skill or task content of jobs.

Used in: Deming (2017), Guvenen, Kuruscu, Tanaka and Wiczer (2020), Lise and Postel-Vinay (2020)

Survey of Business Uncertainty (SBU)

Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta, with Steven Davis and Nicholas Bloom · United States · Monthly · Aggregate data public, firm-level microdata on request

Main variables: firms' current sales and employment, and their subjective probability distributions over sales and employment 12 months ahead, giving expected growth and uncertainty; a special question most months.

Used in: Barrero, Bloom, Davis, Meyer and Mihaylov (2022)

Get the data

Survey of Consumer Expectations (SCE)

Federal Reserve Bank of New York · United States · Monthly since 2013, rotating panel of about 1,300 household heads · Public microdata

Main variables: expectations about inflation, the labour market (job loss, job finding, earnings growth) and household finance, often as probability distributions; demographics and numeracy. The Labor Market Survey module, every four months, adds job search, job offers and offer wages, reservation wages, and satisfaction with pay and benefits.

Used in: Mueller, Spinnewijn and Topa (2021), Faberman, Mueller, Şahin and Topa (2022), Mueller and Spinnewijn (2023), Pilossoph and Ryngaert (2024)

Survey of Unemployed Workers in New Jersey

Alan Krueger and Andreas Mueller, Princeton University · United States (New Jersey) · 2009–2010 · Public, with registration

Main variables: an entry survey of 6,025 unemployment insurance recipients (demographics, income and wealth) and a weekly follow-up with 39,201 person-weeks on job search activity, reservation wages and job offers.

Used in: Mueller, Spinnewijn and Topa (2021)

Get the data

Understanding America Study (UAS)

University of Southern California · United States · Probability-based internet panel of about 15,000 people · Public, with registration

Main variables: a wide range of longitudinal survey modules on economic, social and health outcomes, with some linked administrative and wearable-device data.

Used in: Benjamin, Cooper, Heffetz, Kimball and Zhou (2026)
